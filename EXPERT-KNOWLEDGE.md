## CORRECTION FORMULAS — Real Working Solutions

### Green/Muddy Hair
**Problem:** Color went ashy/muddy/green

**Solution:** Add warm tones to neutralize
- **Red, Red-Orange, or Red-Violet** to the color formula
- Counteracts the unwanted green/ash tones

### Over-Toned Purple
**Problem:** Toner deposited too much violet

**Solution:** Add complementary warm tones
- **Yellow-Orange** to the formula
- Balances out excess violet without stripping

### Resistant Brassy Orange
**Problem:** Orange pigment that won't lift out or tone

**Solution:** Cool tones with blue base
- **Blue-Violet and Blue**
- Stronger than standard ash for stubborn warmth

## SHADOW ROOTS & COLOR MELTS

### Mixing Ratios

**Standard Shadow Root:**
- **1:2** — Demi color : Developer

**Color Melts (varies by line):**
- **1:1:1** — Depending on color line specific formulation
- (Some lines use different ratios — know your brand)

### Processing Tips
- Root shadow processes separately before pulling through
- Watch for hard lines — blend zones while wet

## GRAY COVERAGE — Professional Adjustments

### Standard Gray Coverage (Over 50% Gray)

| Factor | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Normal resistant | 1:1 + 10vol developer |
| More resistant | 15vol-20vol developer |
| Using 15vol+ | Formulate **½ shade darker** for complete coverage |
| Processing | **45 minutes** (full time) |

### Why ½ Shade Darker for Higher Developer?
Higher volume lifts while depositing. The extra lift can make final result lighter than intended. Going ½ shade darker compensates for this effect.

## DOUBLE PROCESS RULES

### Root Retouch + Highlights Same Session

**Critical:** Always bump up lightener developer
- Minimum **20vol** for lightener (even if hair seems fine)
- Compensates for the chemical processing already happening on roots

## ETHNIC HAIR FORMULATION

### Same Base Formulation Applies
Ethnicity itself doesn't change the formulation — hair **condition and history** does.

### Relaxed or Keratin-Treated Hair

| Service | Developer Adjustment |
|---------|---------------------|
| Gray coverage | **15vol-20vol** (resistant) |
| Standard gray | 10vol perfect for non-resistant |
| Lightening | **No higher than 20vol** |

### Why Lower Volume on Chemically Treated Hair?
- Hair already compromised by relaxer/keratin
- Higher developer = more damage risk
- 20vol max protects integrity while still achieving lift

## WHEN YOU BREAK THE RULES

### Texture-Based Adjustments
Rules change based on:
- Hair texture
- Previous chemical exposure
- Medical conditions
- Environmental damage (swimmers, sun, etc.)
- Over-processed history

### Highly Compromised Hair
**Rule:** "More than likely would avoid any color services until repair is done"

Integrity first. Color second.

### Virgin Dark Hair — Carmel Effect Without Full Bleach
**The Hack:**
- **High lift color** + **30vol developer**
- Add a **shot of lightener** to the high lift color mixture to increase lift power
- Achieves lighter result without committing to full bleach session

### Direct Color Pigments IN Lightener
**Pro Trick:**
- Add direct color pigments **directly into the lightener mixture**
- Helps **control and neutralize unwanted warmth** during the lifting process
- Pre-tones while lightening (e.g., adding violet pigment to bleach for yellow control)

## KEY PRINCIPLES

1. **Developer choice is about condition, not just target** — compromised hair = lower volume

2. **Gray coverage needs time** — 45 minutes is non-negotiable

3. **When in doubt, go darker** — especially with higher developer

4. **Texture and exposure matter more than ethnicity** — assess the actual hair in your chair

5. **You can add lightener to high lift** — for that extra push without full bleach

6. **Pigments in lightener** — control warmth at the source

---

## SWIMMER/MEDICAL/ENVIRONMENTAL DAMAGE

Special populations need special care:
- Chlorine damage = porous, unpredictable uptake
- Medical conditions = may process faster/slower
- Previous "DIY disasters" = strand test mandatory

**Always assess before applying.**
