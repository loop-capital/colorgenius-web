## The Science: Eumelanin vs. Pheomelanin

Hair color is determined by melanin — tiny granules synthesized in the hair follicle. There are two types:

| Melanin Type | Color Produced | Characteristics |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Eumelanin** | Black-brown | Larger granules, tougher to remove, responsible for dark hair |
| **Pheomelanin** | Red-yellow | Smaller granules, very difficult to bleach out, concentrated in red hair |

**Critical insight from R&D:** "Mousy brown/blonde hair turns very yellow or orange when lifting because it has similar characteristics to red hair — densely packed with both eumelanin AND pheomelanin." This is called the "Celtic Phenomena."

## Underlying Pigment by Hair Type

### Asian Hair

**Structure characteristics:**
- **Shape:** Very round cross-section
- **Diameter:** Thicker than Caucasian hair
- **Melanin:** Dense concentration of large eumelanin granules
- **Lifting difficulty:** Most resistant to lightening
- **Underlying pigment exposed:** Levels 1-4 = red-orange; Levels 5-7 = orange; Levels 8-9 = yellow-orange

**Formulation adjustments for Asian hair:**

| Service | Adjustment | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Lightening | Expect longer processing times | Dense melanin concentration |
| Lightening | May need 40vol or double process | Large granules resist bleaching |
| Cool toning | Use stronger violet at lower levels | Exposes orange, not yellow |
| Ash colors | Never use straight ash on levels 5-6 | Will go muddy/green |
| Target: Ash blonde | Must lift to pale yellow (level 9+) | Any yellow + ash = green |

**Asian hair lifting phases:**
- Level 1-2 (black) → Red appears at level 4-5
- Level 3-4 (dark brown) → Strong red-orange at level 6-7
- Level 5-6 (medium brown) → Orange at level 7-8
- Level 7-8 (light brown) → Yellow-orange at level 9
- Level 9-10 → Pale yellow to white

### African American / Afro-Textured Hair

**Structure characteristics:**
- **Shape:** Flat, ribbon-like cross-section (elliptical)
- **Texture:** Coarse with tightly coiled pattern
- **Porosity:** Often low porosity (resistant cuticle) or high porosity from chemical processing
- **Melanin:** Very dense eumelanin, large melanosome size
- **Lifting difficulty:** Extremely resistant when virgin; unpredictable when chemically treated

**Formulation adjustments for Afro-textured hair:**

| Service | Adjustment | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Lightening | Pre-soften with 20vol for 10 min before bleach | Opens resistant cuticle |
| Lightening | Use 30-40vol or bond-building lightener | Dense pigment requires strength |
| Deposit color | May need 20vol on resistant virgin hair | Cuticle doesn't open easily |
| Gray coverage | Use NN (double natural) shades | Resistant hair needs maximum pigment |
| Cool tones | Add warmth to formula | Underlying red shows through easily |

**Porosity considerations:**
- **Low porosity (virgin):** Takes longer to process; use higher developer
- **High porosity (relaxed/color-treated):** Processes fast and dark; use lower developer; may need protein filler

---

### Caucasian Hair

**Structure characteristics:**
- **Shape:** Oval cross-section
- **Diameter:** Fine to medium (varies widely)
- **Melanin:** Lower concentration; mix of eumelanin and pheomelanin
- **Lifting difficulty:** Easiest to lighten; lifts predictably

**Subtypes:**

| Subtype | Characteristics | Formulation Notes |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Fine/Thin** | Processes fast, can darken unexpectedly | Use 10vol for deposit; formulate ½ level lighter |
| **Medium** | Standard processing | Follow manufacturer guidelines |
| **Coarse** | Resistant, takes longer | Use 20vol minimum; pre-soften if needed |
| **Celtic/Red-prone** | Dense pheomelanin | Expect orange during lift; use violet tones |

## Ethnicity-Specific Formulation Cheat Sheet

### When Working on Asian Clients

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Using ash toner on level 7 hair → results in green
- ❌ Expecting lift to blonde in one session → often needs double process
- ❌ Using standard processing time → needs 15-20 min longer

**Best practices:**
- ✅ Lift to pale yellow (level 9+) before any ash toner
- ✅ Use double process: bleach to level 9, THEN tone
- ✅ Add gold/warmth to formulas going darker (prevents muddy results)
- ✅ Expect red stage during lifting — plan violet-neutralizing toner

**Example formulas for Asian hair:**

| Target | Process | Formula |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Ash blonde | Double process | Bleach to level 9 → Redken 9V + 9P + Clear |
| Warm brown | Single process | Level 5 + 6G (¾:¼) + 20vol |
| Caramel balayage | Lighten + tone | Balayage to level 7 → 7NB gloss |

---

### When Working on African American Clients

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Using 10vol on virgin coarse hair → color won't penetrate
- ❌ Straight ash on level 5-6 → muddy/green result
- ❌ Overlapping lightener on previously relaxed hair → breakage

**Best practices:**
- ✅ Assess porosity FIRST (water test or touch)
- ✅ Virgin hair: use 20-30vol for deposit, 40vol for lift
- ✅ Previously relaxed: use demi-permanent, lower developer
- ✅ Always strand test when lifting
- ✅ Use NN shades for gray coverage

**Example formulas for African American hair:**

| Target | Process | Formula |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Jet black | Deposit | Level 1N or 2N + 20vol |
| Dark brown with warmth | Deposit | Level 4N + ¼ 4G + 20vol |
| Lighter brown | Fill + deposit | Fill with 6G demi → 5N + 5G permanent |
| Blonde highlights | Pre-soften + bleach | 20vol 10 min pre-soften → Blondor + 30vol |

---

### When Working on Caucasian Clients

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Using 30vol on fine hair → over-processing, damage
- ❌ Same formula across all textures → uneven results
- ❌ Ignoring natural red pigment → brassy results

**Best practices:**
- ✅ Fine hair: 10-20vol max; coarse: 20-30vol
- ✅ Red-prone hair: expect orange; pre-tone with violet
- ✅ Always account for porosity variations

## The Water Drop Test for Porosity

1. Take a clean strand of hair
2. Place in bowl of room-temperature water
3. Observe:

| Result | Porosity | Formulation Adjustment |
|--------|----------|------------------------|
| Floats | Low (resistant) | Higher developer, longer processing, may need pre-softening |
| Sinks slowly | Normal | Standard processing |
| Sinks immediately | High (porous) | Lower developer, reduce processing time, use protein filler |

## Pro Tips for Ethnic Hair Formulation

1. **Asian hair lifts SLOW but holds color well** — be patient with lightening, but deposit is predictable

2. **Afro-textured hair is the most variable** — always assess condition, porosity, and chemical history

3. **Fine Caucasian hair is easiest to damage** — when in doubt, use lower developer

4. **The "Celtic Phenomena"** — mousy brown/blonde hair has pheomelanin characteristics similar to redheads — it goes YELLOW when lifted, not golden

5. **Black hair (level 1) must lift through red and orange stages** — you cannot skip directly to blonde

6. **All hair lightens to yellow eventually** — the difference is HOW LONG it takes and what stages it passes through
