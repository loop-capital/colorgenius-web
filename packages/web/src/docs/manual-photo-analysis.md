# ColorGenius Manual Photo Analysis Guide

## Overview

The manual photo analysis fallback allows stylists to upload client photos, input hair characteristics, and receive shade recommendations without waiting for automated ML analysis.

**When to use manual analysis:**
- During beta testing (automated ML not yet available)
- When client photos are unclear or low quality
- When stylist wants to compare manual vs automated recommendations
- Quick consultations where full analysis isn't needed

**When to wait for automated analysis:**
- High-quality photos with good lighting
- Complex correction scenarios
- When precise color matching is critical
- Client wants the "AI recommendation"

---

## How to Take Client Photos

### Lighting
- **Natural daylight** is best (near a window, indirect sunlight)
- Avoid: fluorescent lighting, yellow indoor lights, direct harsh sunlight
- Photo should show true hair color without color casts

### Angle & Positioning
- **Top-down angle:** Client holds camera above head, hair falls naturally
- **Side profile:** Shows hair texture, length, and color variation
- **Back of head:** Essential for highlighting and balayage assessments
- Include face in at least one photo for skin tone reference

### Hair Prep
- Clean, dry hair (not wet — color appears darker when wet)
- No products that alter appearance (heavy oils, temporary color sprays)
- Natural parting — don't style away from usual part
- If colored hair, note how long since last color application

### Photo Quality
- Minimum 1080p resolution
- In focus — blurry photos make level determination difficult
- Multiple angles recommended (3-5 photos)
- Include photo of current color packaging if known

## Determining Hair Level from Photos

### The 1-10 Level System

| Level | Color | Key Characteristics |
|-------|-------|---------------------|
| 1 | Black | Maximum pigment, very dark |
| 2 | Very Dark Brown | Near black, brown visible in light |
| 3 | Dark Brown | Rich dark brown, common brunette |
| 4 | Medium Brown | Most common natural level |
| 5 | Light Brown | Dark blonde/light brown border |
| 6 | Dark Blonde | Golden tones visible |
| 7 | Medium Blonde | Clearly blonde, natural warmth |
| 8 | Light Blonde | Pale yellow/golden visible |
| 9 | Very Light Blonde | Pale yellow, minimal pigment |
| 10 | Lightest Blonde | Platinum/pale yellow, nearly white |

### Quick Level Assessment Tips

1. **Look at the roots** — virgin hair at roots shows true level
2. **Check in natural light** — indoor lighting can make hair look 1-2 levels darker
3. **Compare to level chart** — Use the in-app Hair Level Chart for reference
4. **Consider ethnicity** — Natural levels vary by genetic background
5. **Previous color matters** — Box dye results may not match natural level

### Common Mistakes
- ❌ Judging level on wet hair (always appears darker)
- ❌ Using flash photography (creates highlights that hide true level)
- ❌ Looking only at ends (ends are often lighter due to sun/age)
- ❌ Ignoring gray hair (gray reads as lighter/yellow)

## Assessing Hair Condition

| Condition | Signs | Processing Risk |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| **Healthy** | Shiny, strong elasticity, minimal breakage | Low — can handle standard processing |
| **Damaged** | Some split ends, dry, slight breakage | Moderate — use lower developer, monitor |
| **Processed** | Previously colored/permed/relaxed | Moderate — test strand recommended |
| **Overprocessed** | Mushy when wet, gummy texture, severe breakage | High — gentle process only, consider conditioning treatments first |

### Simple Tests
- **Stretch test:** Healthy hair stretches ~30% and returns. Overprocessed hair breaks immediately.
- **Porosity test:** Drop hair in water. Floats = low porosity (healthy). Sinks = high porosity (damaged).

## Determining Skin Undertone

### Vein Test
- **Blue/purple veins** = Cool undertone
- **Green veins** = Warm undertone
- **Both/unclear** = Neutral undertone

### Jewelry Test
- **Silver looks better** = Cool undertone
- **Gold looks better** = Warm undertone
- **Both look good** = Neutral undertone

### How Undertone Affects Color Choice
- **Warm undertone:** Golden, honey, caramel tones complement best
- **Cool undertone:** Ash, platinum, cool violet tones complement best
- **Neutral undertone:** Most colors work; natural tones are safest

## Color Theory Basics

### The Color Wheel
- **Warm colors:** Yellow, orange, red, gold
- **Cool colors:** Blue, violet, green, ash
- **Opposites neutralize:** Blue neutralizes orange, violet neutralizes yellow

### Tone Families
| Tone | Code | Effect | Best For |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Natural | /0 | Neutral base | Gray coverage, natural results |
| Ash | /1 | Cool, smoky | Neutralizing warmth, cool blondes |
| Gold | /3 | Warm, rich | Adding warmth, golden results |
| Red | /5 | Vibrant red | Warm red/copper tones |
| Violet | /7 | Cool purple | Neutralizing yellow, smoky tones |

### Corrector Intensifiers
| Corrector | Code | Neutralizes |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| Ash | 0/11 | Orange, red |
| Matt | 0/22 | Orange, red (stronger than ash) |
| Gold | 0/33 | Ash, green |
| Violet | 0/66 | Yellow, pale |
| Blue | 0/88 | Orange (stronger than ash) |

### Developer Guide
| Volume | Lift | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|
| 10 vol | None (deposit only) | Same level or darker, toning |
| 20 vol | 1-2 levels | Gray coverage, gentle lift |
| 30 vol | 2-3 levels | Moderate lift, healthy hair |
| 40 vol | 3-4 levels | Maximum lift, healthy hair only |

## Common Correction Scenarios

### 1. Orange / Brassy Tones
**Cause:** Underlying warm pigment exposed during lightening
**Solution:** Use ash (A) or blue-based correctors
**Recommended:** 0/11 Ash Intensifier, 0/88 Blue Intensifier
**Mix:** Add 1-2 cm corrector to target shade

### 2. Yellow / Brassy Tones
**Cause:** Pale yellow undertone visible in lighter levels
**Solution:** Use violet (V) or ash (A) correctors
**Recommended:** 0/66 Violet Intensifier, 8/1, 8/7
**Mix:** Add violet intensifier for yellow-neutralizing

### 3. Too Green / Ashy
**Cause:** Over-correction with ash tones on porous hair
**Solution:** Add warmth with gold (G) corrector
**Recommended:** 0/33 Gold Intensifier, 6/3, 7/3
**Mix:** Add gold intensifier to warm up result

### 4. Too Warm / Needs Cooling
**Cause:** Natural warmth overpowering desired cool tone
**Solution:** Use matt (M) or ash (A) to cool down
**Recommended:** 0/22 Matt Intensifier, 0/11 Ash Intensifier
**Mix:** Add matt intensifier for stronger cooling

### 5. Uneven / Patchy Color
**Cause:** Uneven porosity, missed spots, or overlapping color
**Solution:** Reapply to darkest areas first, then lighter areas
**Recommended:** Pre-treat with porosity equalizer
**Process:** Apply to virgin hair first, then colored areas last

### 6. Gray Coverage Issues
**Cause:** Resistant gray hair not taking color
**Solution:** Use natural (N) base shades, higher developer
**Recommended:** 3/0, 4/0, 5/0 with 20-30 vol developer
**Mix:** Add small amount of gold intensifier for stubborn gray

## Beta Best Practices

1. **Always take multiple photos** — Different angles reveal different information
2. **Use the level chart** — Don't guess; compare to the visual guide
3. **Consider condition first** — Damaged hair limits options more than level does
4. **Start conservative** — Better to under-promise and over-deliver
5. **Document everything** — Save analysis results for future reference
6. **Test strand when unsure** — Especially with corrections or damaged hair
7. **Educate the client** — Explain why certain shades are recommended
8. **Trust but verify** — Manual analysis is a tool, not a replacement for expertise

## Quick Reference Card

**For stylists using the manual analysis form:**

1. Upload client photo(s)
2. Select current level (compare to chart)
3. Select desired level (consult with client)
4. Assess condition (healthy/damaged/processed/overprocessed)
5. Determine undertone (warm/cool/neutral)
6. Note any gray hair percentage
7. Check if correction is needed
8. Click "Get Shade Recommendations"
9. Review top recommendations with confidence scores
10. Consider risk level and stylist notes before proceeding

**Confidence Score Guide:**
- **80-100% (Green):** High confidence — recommended shade strongly matches inputs
- **60-79% (Yellow):** Moderate confidence — good match but consider alternatives
- **Below 60% (Orange):** Lower confidence — proceed with caution, consider test strand

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Recommendations seem off | Wrong level selected | Re-check level using chart |
| All recommendations too dark/lift | Level mismatch | Verify current vs desired levels |
| Confidence scores low | Condition too limiting | Consider pre-treatment or gentler approach |
| No corrector recommended | Correction type not specified | Check correction checkbox and select type |
| Gray coverage missing | Gray percentage not set | Toggle gray hair and set percentage |
