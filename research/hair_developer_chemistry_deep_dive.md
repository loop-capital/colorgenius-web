## 1. Developer Volume Chemistry

### 1.1 Hydrogen Peroxide Concentration by Volume

| Developer Volume | H₂O₂ Concentration | Primary Use |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **10 Volume** | 3% H₂O₂ | Deposit-only color, subtle tone adjustment, gray coverage |
| **20 Volume** | 6% H₂O₂ | Lift natural hair 1-2 levels, standard permanent color |
| **30 Volume** | 9% H₂O₂ | Lift 2-3 levels, resistant hair, bleaching with powder |
| **40 Volume** | 12% H₂O₂ | Maximum lift (3-4 levels), high-lift color, off-scalp bleaching |

### 1.2 Molecular-Level Oxidation Mechanism

**The Core Chemistry:**

Hair developer works through oxidative decomposition of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), catalyzed by alkaline conditions (ammonia or MEA). The chemical reaction produces reactive oxygen species that break down melanin pigments.

**Key Reaction Steps:**

1. **Deprotonation** (pH-dependent): At alkaline pH (9-11), hydrogen peroxide deprotonates to form the perhydroxyl anion (HOO⁻), believed to be the primary oxidant of melanin.

2. **Melanin Oxidation Pathways:**
   - Eumelanin (brown-black pigment): Oxidized to colorless degradation products
   - Pheomelanin (red-yellow pigment): More resistant to oxidation; requires stronger agents
   - The indole units in melanin undergo ring-opening reactions

3. **Disulfide Bond Breakage:**
   - Cystine (disulfide bonds, -S-S-) → Cysteic acid (-SO₃H)
   - This causes permanent structural damage to keratin
   - ~18% of hair's amino acid composition is cysteine

### 1.3 Lift Level Relationship

The "volume" designation comes from the volume of oxygen gas (in mL) that 1 mL of developer can liberate at STP. Higher volume = more available oxygen = greater oxidative capacity.

**Oxygen Release Relationship:**
- More oxygen → More oxidation → More melanin breakdown → Higher lift
- Trade-off: More oxygen also = more cuticle swelling = more damage potential

## 2. Oil-Based Developers vs Cream Developers

### 2.1 Alkaline Agent Chemistry

Hair color requires alkaline pH (typically 9-11) to:
1. Swell the cuticle for penetration
2. Deprotonate H₂O₂ (pKa = 11.65)
3. Activate persulfate salts (in bleach)

**Two Primary Alkaline Agents:**

#### Ammonia (NH₃)
- **State:** Gas dissolved in water (ammonium hydroxide NH₄OH)
- **pH:** Higher alkalinity (~11-12)
- **Penetration:** Small molecule (MW 17), penetrates cuticle completely
- **Evaporation:** Volatile; evaporates during processing
- **Cuticle Action:** Opens cuticle scales more aggressively
- **Odor:** Strong, characteristic smell
- **Lift Efficiency:** Superior lightening performance

#### Monoethanolamine (MEA)
- **State:** Liquid/liquid-oil based
- **pH:** Lower than ammonia (~9-10)
- **Penetration:** Larger molecule (MW 61) - 3.5x larger than ammonia
- **Evaporation:** Non-volatile; remains in hair
- **Cuticle Action:** Gentler opening of cuticle scales
- **Odor:** Milder (marketed as "ammonia-free")
- **Lift Efficiency:** Less aggressive; color may appear darker/duller

### 2.2 Oil-Based Developer Formulation

**Composition:**
- MEA as primary alkalizer
- Mineral oil or other oils as carriers
- Lower pH formulation
- Gentler on cuticle structure

**Advantages:**
- Reduced odor
- Less scalp irritation
- Gentler cuticle opening
- Better for sensitive clients

**Limitations:**
- Cannot achieve same lift as ammonia-based
- MEA molecules don't leave hair as easily (requires proper cleansing)
- May cause product buildup over time
- Color may not deposit as vibrantly

### 2.3 Molecular Penetration Differences

| Factor | Ammonia-Based | MEA/Oil-Based |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| Molecular Size | Small (17 g/mol) | Large (61 g/mol) |
| Volatility | High (evaporates) | Low (remains in hair) |
| Cuticle Swelling | Aggressive | Moderate |
| Cortex Penetration | Complete | Partial |
| Lift Capability | Higher | Lower |
| Processing Time | Faster | Slower |

## 3. Bond Builders: Olaplex & B3 Mechanisms

### 3.1 Olaplex Chemistry

**Active Ingredient:** Bis-Aminopropyl Diglycol Dimaleate (also called 1,13-diamino-4,7,10-trioxatridecane)

**Molecular Structure:**
- Ether-diamine compound with maleate groups at each end
- Small molecule that can penetrate hair cortex
- Bifunctional: has reactive groups at both ends

**Mechanism of Action:**

1. **Michael Addition Reaction:**
   - Maleate groups (-COO-CH=CH-COO-) react with thiol (-SH) groups from broken cysteine residues
   - Forms new covalent bridges between sulfur atoms

2. **Disulfide Bond Reconstruction:**
   - Target: Broken disulfide bonds (-S-S- → -SH + HS-)
   - Olaplex molecule forms synthetic disulfide bridges
   - Creates longer, stable bonds between cysteine residues

3. **Real-Time Protection:**
   - Can be added directly to bleach/color mixture
   - Bonds form during chemical processing
   - Acts preventively and reparatively

**Stability Note (from PMC5931532):**
- Olaplex active degrades ~17.5% when mixed with bleach over 40 minutes
- Reaction with persulfate forms azanyl ester derivatives
- This is considered during safety evaluation

### 3.2 B3 Brazilian Bond Builder Chemistry

**Mechanism:** Amino acid fusion technology

**Key Approach:**
- Uses amino acids (building blocks of protein)
- Penetrates hair shaft
- Fuses amino acids to weakened keratin fibers
- Specifically targets polypeptide chain reconstruction

**Chemical Process:**
- Provides amino acid building blocks for keratin repair
- Helps reform damaged polypeptide chains
- Creates both covalent and hydrogen bond reinforcement

**Benefits:**
- Rebuilds protein structure
- 12-wash longevity (demi-permanent)
- Seals cuticle to prevent moisture loss
- Contains phospholipids and plant extracts for conditioning

### 3.3 Bond Builder Comparison

| Feature | Olaplex | B3 Brazilian Bond Builder |
|---------|---------|---------------------------|
| **Mechanism** | Disulfide bond bridging | Amino acid fusion |
| **Chemistry** | Synthetic dimaleate bridges | Protein building blocks |
| **Primary Target** | Broken -S-S- bonds | Damaged polypeptide chains |
| **Bond Types** | Disulfide only | Disulfide + peptide |
| **Application** | In-process additive | Demi-permanent treatment |
| **Longevity** | Until next wash | ~12 washes |
| **Active Stability** | Degrades in bleach (~17.5%) | Stable in conditioner base |

### 3.4 K18 (Reference Comparison)

**Mechanism:** Bioactive peptide technology (sh-Oligopeptide-78)

- Mimics human hair keratin type II sequence
- Decapeptide (10-15 amino acids) biomimetic peptide
- Penetrates cortex and reconnects broken keratin chains
- Becomes part of hair structure - hair "recognizes" it as native
- Rebuilds protein skeleton, not just disulfide bonds
- Silicone-free formulation

**Key Difference:**
- Olaplex/B3 = repair broken bonds
- K18 = rebuilds protein structure (polypeptide chains)

## 4. Developer+Bleach vs High-Lift Tint Science

### 4.1 The Fundamental Difference

| Aspect | Bleach + Developer | High-Lift Color |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Primary Agent** | Persulfate salts + H₂O₂ | Ammonia + H₂O₂ |
| **pH** | Higher (10-11) | Moderate (9-10) |
| **Lift Capability** | Unlimited (continuous oxidation) | Max 4 levels |
| **Stopping Point** | None - keeps processing | Self-limiting |
| **Melanin Target** | Complete destruction | Partial degradation |
| **Damage Profile** | Higher (more aggressive) | Lower |

### 4.2 Bleach Chemistry Deep Dive

**Composition:**
- **Persulfate salts:** Ammonium persulfate ((NH₄)₂S₂O₈), potassium persulfate, or sodium persulfate
- **Developer:** Hydrogen peroxide (20-40 vol typical)
- **Alkalizer:** Ammonia or MEA
- **Boosters:** Often contain silica, conditioners

**Persulfate Oxidation Mechanism:**

1. **Persulfate Activation:**
   - S₂O₈²⁻ + heat/alkali → 2 SO₄•⁻ (sulfate radicals)
   - Highly reactive radicals attack melanin

2. **Synergistic Effect:**
   - Persulfate + H₂O₂ work together
   - Persulfate generates radicals
   - H₂O₂ provides sustained oxidation
   - Creates powerful, continuous oxidative environment

3. **Continuous Processing:**
   - No "saturation point" like high-lift
   - Will keep oxidizing until:
     - Product is removed
     - Hair structure fails
     - All melanin destroyed

### 4.3 High-Lift Color Chemistry

**Composition:**
- **Ammonia:** Higher concentration than standard color
- **Developer:** 30-40 volume H₂O₂
- **Direct Dyes:** Toning pigments included
- **Conditioners:** More than bleach

**Mechanism:**

1. **Limited Oxidation:**
   - Only H₂O₂ (no persulfate)
   - Oxidizes melanin but at slower rate
   - Maximum 4 levels of lift before "exhaustion"

2. **Self-Limiting Nature:**
   - Reaches chemical equilibrium
   - Cannot continue beyond 4 levels
   - Toning pigments deposit simultaneously

3. **Melanin Target:**
   - Partial degradation of melanin
   - Eumelanin more affected than pheomelanin
   - Some pigment remains (warmth)

### 4.4 When to Use Each

**Use Bleach When:**
- Lifting >4 levels needed
- Working on previously colored hair
- Need platinum/white blonde
- Removing artificial color
- Full decolorization required

**Use High-Lift When:**
- Virgin hair level 6 or lighter
- Only 3-4 levels lift needed
- Minimizing damage is priority
- Toning in one step
- Natural-looking blonde result acceptable

### 4.5 Melanin Behavior

**Eumelanin (Brown-Black):**
- More susceptible to oxidation
- Breaks down to colorless products
- "Lifts cleaner" - less warmth

**Pheomelanin (Red-Yellow):**
- More resistant to oxidation
- Requires stronger/longer oxidation
- Leaves warm undertones
- Why dark hair goes orange before blonde

## Summary: Key Takeaways

1. **Developer Volume** = H₂O₂ concentration, directly correlates to oxidative power and lift capability

2. **Oil-based developers** (MEA) are gentler but less effective; ammonia-based provides superior lift through smaller molecular size and complete cuticle penetration

3. **Olaplex** works through Michael addition chemistry, creating synthetic disulfide bridges between cysteine residues

4. **B3** fuses amino acids to rebuild keratin polypeptide chains

5. **Bleach** contains persulfate salts for continuous oxidation (unlimited lift); **high-lift** relies only on H₂O₂ (max 4 levels, self-limiting)

6. **Melanin chemistry:** Eumelanin oxidizes more readily than pheomelanin, explaining the "brassiness" of lifted dark hair
