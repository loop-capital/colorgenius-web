# Focus 6: Professional Formulation Secrets — Technical Research Notes

## Executive Summary

This document provides a deep-dive technical analysis of professional hair color formulation secrets, covering the chemistry of warm fillers for gray coverage, ash-on-gray neutralization, gold additive formulations, neutral/NN series double-pigment loading, and the molecular distinctions between gloss and glaze treatments.

---

## 1. Warm Fillers for Gray Coverage Chemistry

### 1.1 The Missing Pigment Problem

Gray hair presents a unique challenge because it has **completely lost its natural melanin** (both eumelanin and pheomelanin). According to the International Association of Trichologists, melanin serves two critical functions:

1. **Provides base color** — the foundation hue visible in the hair
2. **Acts as a chemical anchor** — binding sites for oxidative dye molecules

Without melanin, hair dye has limited molecules to bond to, resulting in transparent, washed-out, or rejected color deposits.

### 1.2 Structural Differences in Gray Hair

Gray hair is not simply "unpigmented hair" — it exhibits distinct structural changes:

| Characteristic | Pigmented Hair | Gray Hair |
|---------------|----------------|-----------|
| Cuticle Structure | Normal scale pattern | More compact, tighter scales |
| Cuticle Penetration | Standard | Resistant, difficult entry |
| Internal pH | Normal (~4.5-5.5) | Altered, affects dye oxidation |
| Porosity (pre-treated) | Even distribution | Irregular, patchy |
| Pigment Binding Sites | Abundant | Absent |

*Source: Blendsor.com pre-pigmentation guide, International Association of Trichologists*

### 1.3 Pre-Pigmentation Chemistry

**Pre-pigmentation** (also called "warm filling") is the process of depositing artificial warm pigments onto white hair before applying the final target color.

#### Scientific Mechanism:
- **Purpose**: Create an artificial pigment layer that mimics natural melanin's role as a chemical anchor
- **Application**: Direct dye application **without developer** or with very low-volume developer (6-9 vol)
- **Processing**: 10-20 minutes with heat, 20-30 minutes without
- **Critical Step**: Do NOT rinse before applying final color — the pre-pigment acts as a foundation

#### Warm Pigment Families by Target Level:

| Target Level | Pre-Pigment Family | Tone Code | Example Formula |
|-------------|-------------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Level 4-5 (Dark Brown) | Red-Copper | .44 / .4 | 5.4 or 4.44 w/o developer |
| Level 5-6 (Medium Brown) | Copper-Gold | .3 / .34 | 6.3 or 5.34 w/o developer |
| Level 6-7 (Light Brown) | Gold-Copper | .34 / .43 | 7.34 or 6.43 w/o developer |
| Level 7-8 (Dark Blonde) | Gold | .3 / .31 | 8.3 or 7.3 w/o developer |

*Source: Blendsor.com Professional Pre-Pigmentation Guide*

### 1.4 When Pre-Pigmentation is Required

| Gray Percentage | Pre-Pigmentation Requirement |
|-----------------|------------------------------|
| Under 30% | Not needed — natural pigmented hair provides anchor |
| 30-50% | Optional — recommended if grays show resistance |
| 50-70% | Recommended — high probability of transparent result without it |
| Over 70% | Required — result will likely fail without pre-pigmentation |
| 100% (Fully Gray) | Always required — no natural melanin for support |

*Source: Blendsor.com Technical Documentation*

## 2. Ash on Gray Chemistry: Violet/Blue Base Neutralization

### 2.1 Color Theory Foundation

Ash formulations operate on the principles of **complementary color neutralization**:

| Undesired Tone | Neutralizing Pigment | Result |
|---------------|---------------------|--------|
| Yellow/Gold | Violet (Purple) | Neutral/Cool Blonde |
| Orange | Blue | Cool Brown/Ash |
| Red-Orange | Blue-Violet | Ash Brown |
| Brassy/Yellow-Orange | Blue + Violet mix | Clean Ash |

*Source: Ugly Duckling Color Theory, PJ's College Cosmetology Guide*

### 2.2 Ash Formulation Chemistry on Gray Hair

When applying ash tones to gray hair, the chemistry differs from pigmented hair:

1. **No Underlying Warmth to Counteract**: Gray hair lacks the natural warm undertones (pheomelanin) that typically counterbalance ash pigments
2. **Pure Ash Deposition**: Without warm filler, ash pigments deposit "too true," often appearing harsh, greenish, or unnatural
3. **The Green Risk**: Blue-based ash applied to yellow/orange residual pigment can create green (blue + yellow = green)

### 2.3 Professional Formulation Ratios

Based on professional color line recommendations (Ugly Duckling, Framesi):

| Desired Correction | Additive | Ratio |
|-------------------|----------|-------|
| Neutralize yellow/gold | Violet additive | 1/2 inch per tube of base color |
| Neutralize orange/brass | Blue additive (Ash Blue) | 3/4 inch per tube of base color |
| Extreme brass correction | Blue + Grey additive | 3/4 inch Blue per tube |

*Source: Ugly Duckling Professional Additive Guidelines*

### 2.4 Ash Blue Additive Chemistry

**Ash Blue Additive** is specifically formulated to counteract orange tones that appear when lifting darker hair levels:

- **Target**: Red-orange and orange pigment exposed during lightening
- **Mechanism**: Blue pigment molecules bond to hair fiber, neutralizing orange through complementary color cancellation
- **Use Case**: Level 4 brown or darker lifted to lighter levels
- **Result**: Silvery, clean tone without turning icy violet

### 2.5 Gray Hair Specific Considerations

For gray hair specifically, ash application requires:

1. **Pre-pigmentation with warm base** before ash application (prevents muddy/green results)
2. **Lower developer volume** (10-20 vol) to prevent over-processing
3. ** shorter processing time** (resistant gray may process differently)
4. **Strand testing essential** — gray hair response to ash is unpredictable

## 3. Gold Additives: Yellow Pigment Formulation for Brassiness Correction

### 3.1 The Gold Additive Purpose

Gold additives (.3 / .33 family) serve multiple functions:

1. **Warmth Enhancement**: Adds golden reflect to cool or neutral bases
2. **Brassiness Management**: Counterintuitively, controlled gold can PREVENT uncontrolled brass
3. **Gray Coverage Support**: Provides the missing warm foundation that gray hair lacks
4. **Color Correction**: Adjusts tones that have pulled too ash or green

### 3.2 Chemistry of Gold Pigments

**Golden Violet (.32) Additive** represents an advanced formulation:

- **Dual-Action Pigment**: Combines gold (warmth) with violet (cooling) in balanced ratios
- **Brassiness Elimination**: Violet component neutralizes unwanted yellow while gold maintains warmth
- **Target Shades**: Dark brown through medium blonde (levels 4-7)

*Source: Alfaparf Milano Professional Pigments Technical Data*

### 3.3 Ultra-Concentrated Pigment Technology

Modern gold additives utilize **pure ultra-concentrated pigments** that are:

- **Intermixable**: Can be added to any pH-compatible product (shampoo, conditioner, mask, mousse, gel, paste)
- **Versatile**: Used in oxidative color, direct dye, or maintenance products
- **Highly Concentrated**: Requires minimal volume for significant tonal shift

**Application Matrix:**

| Product Type | Gold Additive Function |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Oxidative Color | Primary pigment deposit |
| Shampoo/Conditioner | Maintenance and toning |
| Mask/Treatment | Deep conditioning + color deposit |
| Styling Products (mousse/gel) | Surface color refresh |

*Source: Alfaparf Milano Professional Pigments Documentation*

### 3.4 Color Correction Applications

**Correcting Unwanted Warmth with Gold:**

While gold adds warmth, it's also used strategically to CORRECT over-ashed hair:

- **Scenario**: Hair has pulled "too ash" or green
- **Solution**: Add controlled gold (.3) to warm without creating brass
- **Mechanism**: Introduces the warm reflect that was over-neutralized

## 4. Neutral Formulation: N Series vs. NN (Double Neutral)

### 4.1 The N Series: Natural/Neutral

**N (Natural)** series colors are formulated with:

- **Balanced Pigment Composition**: Equal parts warm and cool tones
- **Neutral Undertones**: Avoid excessive warmth or coolness
- **Gray Coverage**: Effective for standard gray coverage (up to 50%)
- **Blending Ability**: Seamless integration with existing hair color

**N Series Characteristics:**
- True-to-life color mimicking natural hair pigments
- Versatile across skin tones
- Safe choice for subtle color changes
- Standard gray coverage efficacy

### 4.2 The NN Series: Intense Natural / Double Natural

**NN (Double Natural)** represents an enhanced formulation:

| Characteristic | N Series | NN Series |
|---------------|----------|-----------|
| Pigment Concentration | Standard | 2x (double load) |
| Gray Coverage | Up to 50% | Up to 100% (resistant gray) |
| Color Intensity | Natural | Rich, saturated |
| Longevity | Standard | Extended |
| Best For | Standard gray, natural look | Resistant gray, intense coverage |

*Source: CookedByTaste Technical Analysis, Chromastics Color Manual*

### 4.3 Double-Pigment Loading Chemistry

**"Double N" or NN** formulations contain:

1. **Enhanced Pigment Load**: Twice the concentration of standard N series
2. **Ammonia Adjustment**: Often higher ammonia content to open resistant cuticles
3. **Developer Recommendation**: Typically 20-volume (6%) developer for resistant gray

**Chromastics Technical Manual specifies:**
> "Neutral concentrate is used to create 'double N' colors for better gray coverage... Use the following guidelines when adding concentrates to formulas."

### 4.4 Professional Application Guidelines

**Resistant Gray Formulation (Clairol Professional):**

| Client Profile | Formula | Developer |
|---------------|---------|-----------|
| Natural Brunette, 50% resistant gray, target medium golden brown | 1 oz 5NN + 1 oz 5GN | 2 oz 20 vol |
| Natural Blonde, 25% resistant gray, target warm golden blonde | 1.5 oz 7A + 2 oz 7NN | 2 oz 20 vol |
| 50% gray, target rich red | 1 oz 6NN + 1 oz 4RR | 2 oz 20 vol |

*Source: Clairol Professional Resistant Gray Coverage Formulation Guide*

## 5. Gloss vs. Glaze: Molecular Differences

### 5.1 Hair Gloss (Demi-Permanent)

**Definition**: A demi-permanent color treatment that penetrates the hair cuticle

#### Molecular Mechanism:

| Feature | Gloss (Demi-Permanent) |
|---------|----------------------|
| Cuticle Action | Gently opens outer layer for penetration |
| Pigment Deposit | Inside cuticle layer, partial cortex penetration |
| Developer Required | Yes (low-volume, typically 6-10 vol) |
| Ammonia Content | Low to none (alkaline agents like MEA may be used) |
| pH Characteristic | Alkaline application, returns to acidic after processing |
| Molecular Bonding | Partial oxidation, limited polymerization |

**Duration**: 24-28 washes

*Source: Wella Professionals Technical Documentation, Madison Reed*

### 5.2 Hair Glaze (Semi-Permanent / Temporary)

**Definition**: A temporary coating treatment that sits on the hair surface

#### Molecular Mechanism:

| Feature | Glaze (Semi-Permanent) |
|---------|----------------------|
| Cuticle Action | No cuticle opening — surface coating only |
| Pigment Deposit | Adheres to outer cuticle layer, no penetration |
| Developer Required | No developer |
| Ammonia/Alkaline Agents | None — ammonia-free, peroxide-free |
| pH Characteristic | Neutral to slightly acidic (cuticle-sealing) |
| Molecular Bonding | Physical adsorption, no chemical reaction |

**Duration**: 1-2 weeks (4-8 washes)

*Source: Davines Technical Guide, Byrdie Beauty Science*

### 5.3 Molecular Comparison Table

| Property | Gloss (Demi-Permanent) | Glaze (Semi-Permanent) |
|----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Cuticle Penetration** | Partial — opens outer layer | None — surface only |
| **Pigment Location** | Inside cuticle | On cuticle surface |
| **Oxidation Required** | Yes (with low-volume developer) | No |
| **Lift Capability** | Zero lift (deposit only) | Zero lift |
| **Gray Coverage** | Up to 70% (with Color Touch Plus) | None — blending only |
| **Damage Profile** | Low damage | Zero damage |
| **Processing Time** | 15-30 minutes | 5-20 minutes |
| **Fade Pattern** | Gradual, even fade | Quick washout |
| **Best For** | Toning, gray blending, shine | Instant shine, temporary color |

*Source: Wella Professionals, Madison Reed, Davines, Byrdie*

### 5.4 SHINEFINITY Color Glaze Technology (Case Study)

**Advanced Demi-Glaze Technology** demonstrates the boundary between gloss and glaze:

- **pH-Balanced Technology**: Gently opens cuticle for color entry, then pH drops to reseal
- **Zero Lift, Zero Damage**: Ammonia-free, peroxide-free
- **Processing**: 20 minutes standard, 5 minutes for "flash glaze"
- **Result**: Glass-like shine with translucent veil of color

*Source: Wella Professionals SHINEFINITY Technical Documentation*

## 6. Resistant Gray: Pre-Softening Chemistry

### 6.1 Pre-Softening Definition

**Pre-softening** is a chemical process applied BEFORE color to resistant gray hair:

- **Purpose**: Open compact cuticle scales to allow color penetration
- **Agent**: Alkaline-based products (typically ammonia orMEA solutions)
- **Method**: Apply 20-volume developer to resistant areas first, then color

### 6.2 Pre-Softening vs. Pre-Pigmentation

| Technique | Purpose | Chemistry | Timing |
|-----------|---------|-----------|--------|
| Pre-Softening | Open cuticle structure | Alkaline (developer, ammonia) | Before color, 5-10 min |
| Pre-Pigmentation | Deposit warm foundation | Direct dye, no developer | Before color, 10-20 min |

*Source: HairFinder.com Technical Guide, Dennis Bernard Professional*

### 6.3 Professional Pre-Softening Protocol

**Dennis Bernard G.O.D. (Gray Oxidizing Drops):**
- Contains all three primary pigments
- Exact ammonia concentration to gently open cuticle
- Added directly to formulation

**Application:**
1. Apply 20-volume developer to resistant hairline/areas
2. Wait 5-10 minutes (do not process fully)
3. Apply color formulation directly over pre-softening agent
4. Process normally

## 7. Summary: Formulation Decision Matrix

| Problem | Solution | Chemistry Basis |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| Resistant gray, transparent color | Pre-pigmentation + NN series | Warm filler creates anchor; double pigment provides coverage |
| Gray hair, ash desired | Pre-pigmentation first, then ash | Warm foundation prevents green/muddy results |
| Brassy orange tones | Blue additive (3/4 inch) | Complementary color neutralization |
| Brassy yellow tones | Violet additive (1/2 inch) or Gold-Violet | Violet neutralizes yellow; gold maintains warmth |
| Need shine + tone, low commitment | Glaze | Surface coating, no penetration |
| Need shine + tone, longer lasting | Gloss (demi-permanent) | Cuticle penetration, 24-28 washes |
| Resistant cuticle, color rejection | Pre-softening | Alkaline cuticle opening |
